# LinkedIn writing

## Contract

A post is read on a phone, in a feed, by someone who did not ask for it. It earns the second line with the first, and it never claims what the source does not say.

## Rules

1. **Facts from the source, numbers from the primary doc.** Features come from the repo itself (README, commit bodies, the shared docs), never from memory. A price, a limit or a multiplier is checked against the vendor's own docs before it is written; one that was not checked is named as unchecked.
2. **Ownership is the author's call.** No "I built", no product or repo name, no account handle, no install line unless the brief allows it. When unsure, write it neutral and put the link in the first comment.
3. **Hook, mechanism, payoff, question.** First line is the problem the reader has, not the thing that was made. Then how it works, then why it matters, then one question that invites a comment.
4. **Posts are plain text.** The composer renders no Markdown: no `**`, no `#` headings, no backticks. Emoji or arrows carry the bullets. Articles take real headings, lists and bold.
5. **A worked number beats an adjective.** "90k × 0.1 vs 90k × 2: twenty times the price" over "much cheaper".
6. **Five to seven hashtags, at the end.**
7. **Never publish.** Filling a draft is fine; the Post, Publish and Schedule buttons are the user's alone.
